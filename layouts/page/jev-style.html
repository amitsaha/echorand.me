One cutoff for extra care

A clinic offers a care-management program to patients whose risk score is at or above a cutoff. Patients below the line get routine care. These sixteen patients have the same kind of illness. The score predicts what the clinic will spend on them. Less is spent on Black patients who have the same need, so their scores sit lower.

The cutoff never reads the group. Because the scores sit in different places, that one line offers the program to fewer Black patients. These scores are written into the page so you can move the line. The same mechanism, a cost score and one enrollment cutoff, is what Obermeyer et al. reported in Science in 2019: at a given score, Black patients were sicker, and the cutoff identified fewer of them for extra care.
